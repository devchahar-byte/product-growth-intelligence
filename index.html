Dataset Architecture & Source Documentation

This platform is grounded in verifiable event-level e-commerce data from two authoritative sources:

1. Real Production E-Commerce Event Stream (Bundled)

Direct public clickstream record from an operational retail store, containing 9,381 event actions across 5,000 unique customer journeys.

  • Direct Raw Source: Download Raw CSV (575 KB)
  • Source Repository: prachi-ps/ecommerce-funnel-analysis
  • Local Repository Path: data/real_ecommerce_events.csv
  • Tracked Dimensions: event_id, user_id, event_type, event_date, product_id, amount, traffic_source, device_category.
2. Google Analytics 4 Official E-Commerce Benchmark

Exported event telemetry from Google's flagship Merchandise Store, covering millions of sessions with nested schemas.

  • Official Documentation: Google Developers GA4 BigQuery Demo Dataset
  • BigQuery Table Reference: bigquery-public-data.ga4_obfuscated_sample_ecommerce.events_*
  • Temporal Coverage: November 1, 2020 through January 31, 2021 (92 days).

How It Works & Metric Significance

Understanding what each metric represents, how it is derived, and what action the product growth team should take:

1. Conversion Funnel & Drop-off Leakages

Definition: The percentage of unique users who advance from one critical step to the next. Calculated as:
Step Drop-off % = (1 - (Sessions at Stage N / Sessions at Stage N-1)) * 100.
Significance: Identifies where product friction causes abandonment. A high drop from View Item to Cart signals pricing resistance, whereas a high drop from Checkout to Purchase indicates payment friction.

2. The Device Disparity (Mobile vs. Desktop CVR)

Definition: Ratio of desktop conversion rate to mobile conversion rate.
Significance: Mobile accounts for ~60% of traffic on modern sites. A 2x disparity is common due to checkout fatigue (typing address and credit cards on small screens). This represents the highest-leverage opportunity for single-tap payment integration (Apple Pay / Google Pay).

3. Statistical Significance & A/B Sample Sizing

Definition: We compute two-proportion Z-tests with 95% confidence intervals and Wilson score estimation.
Significance: Prevents false-positive decisions. Historical GA4 data cannot prove causation — only an A/B test with adequate statistical power (80%) and sample size can validate whether an intervention truly drove growth.
